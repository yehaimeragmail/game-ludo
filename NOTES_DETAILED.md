## Game Notes — as if nobody has ever played this game

Imagine a square board game where four little coloured teams race around a track, like children running around a playground, and the first team to get every single runner safely home wins the race. That is Ludo. You do not need to know anything about board games before you open it. This game is the Artisan Parlor edition, so the board looks like a warm wooden parlour table, with soft felt colours and shiny little tokens shaped like pawns, gems, stars, crowns, and other pretty shapes, depending on the token style you pick. Nothing moves on its own. You roll a dice, you choose which token to move, and you watch it hop along the track.

When the game first opens, you see a short splash screen that says Ludo and THE ARTISAN PARLOR EDITION, then a loading line that says Ready! After that you land on the main menu. At the top is the big word Ludo. Below it is a large Play button. Under Play you will find a row of small round buttons for Share, Rate, Settings, How to Play, and a button for Support Wajiha and the Pro screen. The menu also shows a Game Mode card, and that card is where the real choices live, so take your time there before you press Play.

On the Game Mode card, first choose how many players will play. You can pick 2, 3, or 4 players. Next, for each seat, choose Human or Bot. A Human seat is played by a person tapping on this phone. A Bot seat is played by the phone itself, and you will see the word BOT beside that player during the game. So a two-player game could be you against one Bot, or two people sharing the phone and taking turns tapping. A four-player game could be one person against three Bots, or four people passing the phone around. After that, choose the Bot difficulty: Easy, Medium, or Hard. Easy Bots make simple, friendly moves. Medium Bots think a little more. Hard is a Pro feature, so if you do not have Pro unlocked, Hard shows a lock symbol and tapping it will take you to the Pro screen instead of starting a Hard game. Then you can pick your Parlor Style, which is the board theme, your token style, which is the shape of your little playing pieces, and your dice style, which is how the dice looks. You can also tap a player name and type a new name, so instead of Player 1 you could be called Amna, and instead of Player 2 the Bot could be called Bruno. The four colours in a normal game are the four parlor colours shown on the board, one colour per player, and each player starts with four tokens sitting in their own coloured corner base, which is like a little home yard where the tokens wait before the race starts.

To start, set your players, your Bot difficulty, your theme, and your names, then tap Play. The board appears. You will see the square track running around the outside of the board, with a coloured home column for each player leading into the middle, and a home triangle right in the centre. Each player has four tokens. At the top or side of the board you will see whose turn it is, written as a short message, for example "Tap the dice, Amna!" That message is your best friend in this game. Always read it before you tap anything. When it is your turn, tap the dice. The dice tumbles and shows a number from 1 to 6. If the game says "Tap a glowing token!" then one or more of your tokens is glowing, and you must tap one of those glowing tokens to move it. Tokens that cannot move do not glow, so you cannot pick a wrong one by accident at that step, but you still have to think about which glowing token is the cleverest one to move.

Here is the heart of the game, step by step, in the order it happens. First, you roll the dice. Second, if you roll a 6 and you still have a token waiting in your base, you may bring one token out onto the track. Rolling a 6 is the only way a token leaves its base. If you roll a 1, 2, 3, 4, or 5 while all your tokens are still in the base, you cannot move, and your turn passes to the next player. Third, once a token is on the track, it moves clockwise around the board, hopping forward exactly as many squares as the dice shows. For example, if Amna rolls a 4 and taps her red token, that token hops forward four squares along the track. Fourth, after your token moves, the game checks what happened on the square where it landed. Fifth, the turn either stays with you for an extra roll, or it passes to the next player, and the next player rolls.

Now the special rules that make Ludo exciting, and you must learn them one by one. Rolling a 6 gives you another roll. Capturing a rival gives you another roll. Bringing a token all the way home gives you another roll. So a lucky turn can keep going for several rolls in a row. But there is a catch, and it is a strict one: if you roll three sixes in a row, your turn is forfeited, and the game shows the message "Three sixes! Turn forfeited." That means your third six does not count as a move at all, and play passes on. So two sixes in a row feel wonderful, but the third six is a trap, and good players remember how many sixes they have rolled in this turn.

Capturing works like this. If your token lands exactly on a square where a rival token is standing, and that square is not a safe square, you capture that rival token, and it is sent all the way back to its own base, where it must wait for a 6 before it can come out again. The game will announce it, for example "Capture! Amna sends a rival home!" Safe squares are marked with a star on the board, and tokens standing on a safe square cannot be captured, no matter who lands there. That is why experienced players love to park a token on a star square when a rival is close behind. If two rival tokens land on the same ordinary square, the later one captures the earlier one, so never assume a square is safe just because you arrived first, unless it has a star.

To win, a token must travel all the way around the track, then turn into its own coloured home column, then hop up that column and land exactly in the centre home triangle. Exact means exact. If your token is two squares away from home and you roll a 5, it cannot move into home, because it would overshoot. You must wait for a roll of exactly 2, or move a different token instead. The game shows "Token home! Roll again!" when a token does reach home, and you get another roll. The first player to bring all four tokens home wins the whole game. There is no second place prize inside one game. The end screen says Victory! for the winner, or Game Over for the others in a Bot game, with buttons for Play Again and Main Menu. Play Again starts a fresh game with the same setup. Main Menu takes you back to the menu screen.

Here is a worked example with made-up names, so you can picture a real game. Amna plays red, Bruno the Bot plays blue, and there are only two players. Amna rolls a 6, brings a red token out of her base, and rolls again because a 6 gives an extra roll. She rolls a 3, moves that token three squares forward, and her turn ends. Bruno rolls a 6, brings a blue token out, rolls again, rolls a 2, and moves two squares. Later Amna rolls a 5, and her red token lands exactly on Bruno's blue token on an ordinary square, not a star square, so Bruno's blue token flies back to its base, Amna gets another roll, and Bruno must roll a 6 before that blue token can race again. Much later Amna has three red tokens home and one token two squares from home. She rolls a 2, that last token hops home, and Amna wins, because all four of her tokens are home before Bruno's are.

If you make a wrong move, meaning a move you later wish you had not made, Ludo does not let you take it back. There is no Undo button in the game screen. Once you tap a glowing token, that token moves, and the game carries on. The only way to undo a bad game is to start over. During a game, tap the pause control to open the pause panel, which offers Resume, Restart, and Quit to Menu. Resume carries on exactly where you stopped. Restart throws the current game away and starts a fresh game. Quit to Menu leaves the game and goes back to the main menu. If you tap a token that is not glowing, nothing happens, and the board does not change, so a stray tap cannot ruin your game. If you roll and no token can move, the game tells you and passes the turn on, which is not a punishment, just a quiet turn.

Sound and look are controlled from Settings, reached from the main menu. Settings has switches for Music, Sound effects, and a Volume slider. Music is the gentle background tune. Sound effects are the little clicks, dice sounds, and celebration sounds when you capture or win. Turning Music off stops the tune but leaves the clicks on, and turning Sound effects off stops the clicks but leaves the music on, so set each one the way you like it. You can also change the Theme, the Token style, and the Dice style in Settings. There is a Theme Creator screen, called Custom Theme, where you can build your own board colours and press Use This Theme to play on your own creation. The Pro screen explains the difference between Free and Pro: Pro unlocks Hard mode, extra board accents, and more themes, token shapes, and dice styles. The Apple Pro purchase is called ludopro, and it is a one-time unlock in the Apple version.

Saving works quietly in the background. Your settings, your chosen theme, your token and dice styles, your player names, and your Pro unlock are saved on your device, so when you close the game and open it again, your choices are still there. Check in the game whether a half-finished Ludo match is still waiting for you after you quit to the menu or close the app completely, because the menu does not promise a Resume Game button for Ludo in the same way some other games do, and you should not assume a long match will wait for you overnight. To exit cleanly, pause the game, tap Quit to Menu, and then leave the app from your phone's normal home control. If you are in the middle of a Bot game and you simply switch to another app, the game pauses its music, and when you come back you should find the board where you left it during that same session, but a fresh Play press always starts a brand new match.

## What to Test — for TestFlight

Start on the main menu and check that the title, the Play button, and the Game Mode card all appear, and that Share, Rate, Settings, How to Play, and the Pro button all open without a blank screen. Set the game to 2 players, you as Human and one Bot on Easy, and press Play. Confirm the board shows four tokens per player in their corner bases and a message telling the first player to tap the dice. Roll the dice several times and confirm the dice number matches the number of squares the token hops. Test the 6 rule on purpose: keep rolling until you get a 6, bring a token out, and confirm you get another roll. Then test a non-6 roll with all tokens in base at the start of a fresh game, and confirm no move is possible and the turn passes on.

Play until you can capture: move a token onto a rival token on an ordinary square and confirm the rival token returns to its base and you get another roll. Then move a token onto a star safe square, let a rival land there too, and confirm no capture happens on the star. Test the three-sixes trap: if you roll six, six, and then six again in one turn, confirm the message "Three sixes! Turn forfeited." appears and the turn passes without a move. Test the exact-home rule: bring a token to two squares from home, roll a larger number, and confirm it cannot enter home, then roll the exact number on a later turn and confirm the "Token home! Roll again!" message.

Test a full win: play a complete game until one player brings all four tokens home, and confirm the Victory or Game Over screen appears with working Play Again and Main Menu buttons. Play Again should start a fresh board, and Main Menu should return to the menu. Test a full loss against a Bot on Easy, then again on Medium, and confirm the Bot actually takes turns, moves tokens, and can win. If you have Pro, test Hard as well, and confirm Hard feels sharper than Easy, and that without Pro, Hard shows a lock and does not start a Hard game. Test 3-player and 4-player setups, with a mix of Human and Bot seats, and confirm every seat gets turns in order and the turn message always names the right player.

Test the wrong-move case: deliberately move a token you wish you had not moved, and confirm there is no Undo and the move stands. Test Restart from the pause panel and confirm the board resets completely. Test Quit to Menu and confirm you land back on the menu. Test sound: turn Music off in Settings, play a turn, and confirm no background tune, then turn it back on and confirm the tune returns. Do the same for Sound effects, and drag the Volume slider low and high to confirm the sound gets quieter and louder. Test exit and reopen: pause a game, quit to the menu, close the app completely, reopen it, and check whether your settings, names, theme, and Pro state are still there, and note honestly whether a half-finished match resumed or started fresh, because that point is marked check in the game in the notes above. Finally test the Theme Creator: build a custom theme, press Use This Theme, start a game, and confirm your colours actually appear on the board.
